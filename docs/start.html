When you can’t make yourself start 🧠

From @techyai_cj. It's almost never the work that's hard, it's the first move. These are the five I actually use. None of them are motivational and none of them ask you to feel differently.

01 · Make the first step embarrassingly small

the one I use most

Most tasks feel impossible because you're holding the whole thing in your head at once. This forces it down to one move.

Copy:

Here's what I have to do: [task]. Break it into the smallest possible first step, something I could finish in under five minutes. Just one step. Don't give me the full plan, don't give me a list, and don't tell me it'll be fine once I get going. One step.

02 · Match the work to the energy you actually have

instead of the energy you wish you had

Some days the answer isn't the important task, it's the one you can actually do right now.

Copy:

I have about [20] minutes and honestly not much energy. Here's my list: [paste]. Tell me the one thing to do right now that fits what I've actually got, not what I should be doing. If the honest answer is that nothing on the list fits, say that and tell me what to do instead.

03 · Get it out of your head one question at a time

for when it's too tangled to explain

Sometimes you can't start because you can't articulate the thing. Don't try to write it all out. Get interviewed instead.

Copy:

I need to do something and I can't get it straight in my head. Ask me one question at a time, waiting for each answer, until you understand what I'm actually trying to do. Don't give advice yet. When you've got it, tell it back to me in three sentences.

04 · Reorder the list so it's doable

your order is usually the problem

Lists get written in the order things occurred to you, which is almost never the order they should be done in.

Copy:

Here's my to-do list: [paste]. Rewrite it in the order I should actually do things, taking into account what blocks what and what's quick. Put one genuinely easy thing first so I get moving. Mark anything that doesn't need doing at all.

05 · Negotiate, don't push through

for the thing you've avoided for a week

This one sounds too simple to work. It works anyway.

Copy:

I've been avoiding [task] for about a week now and I don't fully know why. Ask me two questions to work out what's actually stopping me. Then talk me into doing ten minutes of it, not the whole thing, ten minutes. No pep talk, no motivational language, just make the ten minutes sound manageable.

These work better if your profile is filled in. It stops the answers coming back generic. There's a separate guide for that.

None of this is medical or professional advice. It's just what I use to get moving on days I otherwise wouldn't.
